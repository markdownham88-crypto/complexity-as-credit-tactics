02 · The thinking

From insolvency to asset management

Lending resilience is measured by the most effective loan management and debt recovery solution. Loan management is the recovery of value through the application of asset management techniques to debt recovery: value creation and cost containment, set out in a value recovery plan with outcome statements.

“The real risk management and risk mitigation is the quality and effectiveness of the asset management thinking.” Mark Downham, on complex property recoveries in specialist lending

Mark’s practice treats a distressed loan as a live system. The facility, the asset, the sponsor, the occupiers, the contractor, the valuer and the buyer pool all act on one another, and the route to recovery changes as they do. Complexity thinking here is not theory. It is a disciplined way to see those interactions, test options before committing to them, and choose the move that leaves the lender with the most value and the most room.

The working style matters as much as the method. Mark’s framework is collaborative and facilitative rather than directive and prescriptive: the lender’s team, the receiver and the advisers think the situation through together in a shared space, and the lender decides with all the information in front of it.

The conventional route

  • Wait for the covenant breach or payment default
  • Treat enforcement as one decision: appoint or don’t
  • Assess each loan on its own file
  • Directive, prescriptive advice handed down
  • Insolvency process sets the pace

The Belleveue Mortlakes route

  • Read behavioural signals months before the trigger
  • Climb a ladder of escalation, one evidenced rung at a time
  • Map how loans, counterparties and assets interact
  • Collaborative, facilitative thinking with the lender’s team
  • Asset management sets the pace; receivership only where it is needed
03 · Complexity, translated into tactics

Six field tactics for high-stakes books

Each tactic is stated in the lender’s language. Switch the view to see the complexity concept behind it.

T1Pre-covenant tellsWeak signals

Late reporting, slower sponsor replies, shorter monitoring reports, unpaid subcontractors, an early refinance conversation.

Move: log a monthly “tells” score on every watchlist loan. Two together make a situation.

T2Shared fault linesCoupling and interdependence

The same contractor, valuer, anchor tenant, sponsor family or exit-buyer pool behind loans that look unrelated.

Move: list those names across your top 20 exposures. A name that appears three times is portfolio risk.

T3Cliff edgesNon-linearity and tipping points

The single tenant loss, planning refusal or 25bp move that flips a performing loan into default.

Move: name the one event per loan that would tip it, and who would see it first.

T4Reversible movesSafe-to-fail probes

Information rights, drawdown conditions, a conditional standstill, a quiet look at the buyer pool. Each buys information and keeps routes open.

Move: write the next three moves in order and mark which can be undone.

T5Changing the borrower’s payoffsGame theory

A sponsor’s rational move is often delay. The lender wins by making delay costly and cooperation rewarding, with credible next steps.

Move: tie any extension to dated milestones, with the next rung already prepared.

T6Pattern reading across casesData point patterning

Over 1,000 appointments leave patterns: how a scheme stalls, how value leaks, what a convincing explanation looks like without evidence.

Move: compare a live situation against known failure patterns before choosing a route.
